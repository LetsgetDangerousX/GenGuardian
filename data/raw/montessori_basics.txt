THE ABSORBENT MIND
The child has a different type of mind from ours. It is a mind that absorbs knowledge unconsciously, like a sponge absorbs water. This is called the Absorbent Mind. From birth to age six, children have this incredible ability to learn without effort, simply by living in their environment.

SENSITIVE PERIODS
Children go through specific windows of opportunity called Sensitive Periods:
- Order (age 1-3): Need for consistency and routine
- Language (birth-6): Absorb language effortlessly
- Movement (birth-4): Refining motor skills
- Small Objects (1-4): Fascination with tiny things
- Social Aspects (2.5-6): Learning social skills

THE PREPARED ENVIRONMENT
The classroom or home should be prepared to support the child's independence:
- Child-sized furniture
- Materials on low shelves
- Real tools that work
- Order and beauty
- Freedom to choose

FOLLOWING THE CHILD
Observation is key. Watch what the child is interested in and provide appropriate materials. Don't interrupt concentration.

PRACTICAL LIFE
Activities that help children learn to care for themselves and their environment:
- Pouring water
- Buttoning frames
- Sweeping
- Food preparation
- Polishing

FREEDOM AND DISCIPLINE
True discipline comes from within, not from outside. When children can choose their work freely, they develop self-discipline through concentration.

NORMALIZATION
When children are allowed to concentrate on work they love, they become calm, peaceful, and happy. This state is called normalization.
